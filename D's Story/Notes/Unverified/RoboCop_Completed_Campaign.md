# RoboCop Campaign — Completed Detroit War (Scenes 90–240)

**Record type:** Retrospective canonical events, adopted at the player's direction, not a future outline. **Dates:** November 19, 2033 – April 22, 2034 (in-story). This supersedes the Scene 89 checkpoint. Details not specifically established before this update, particularly event counts and casualty totals, are **in-universe campaign estimates established for this continuation**; they are not claims about the original RoboCop canon. The scene ranges summarize elapsed episodes; they do not imply 151 separately played encounters.

- **Open fighting:** **39 major armed engagements** (sustained firefights, coordinated assaults or district-scale battles) and approximately **210 smaller armed clashes** (gang encounters, patrol attacks, raids, ambushes and isolated exchanges). The largest phase of fighting lasts approximately six weeks; unrest and intermittent violence extend across five months.
- **Estimated deaths:** **2,480** over the entire escalating conflict: about **990 civilians**, **220 police and emergency/security personnel**, and **1,270 gang fighters, corporate combatants and other armed participants**. These are working postwar estimates, not a perfect body-by-body census. Many more injured and displaced. The casualty categories are intended to be mutually exclusive.

D is not credited with every opponent defeated by Ty, Murphy, police, or resistance forces. He does not kill for convenience, and his powerful abilities do not make him omnipresent. Police, civilians, and the other companions act independently.

- **Outcome:** OCP's attempted forcible takeover of Detroit fails; Dick Jones and associated executives face arrests and criminal investigations, with some conspirators still at large. Independent police and municipal services survive, though the city remains damaged. The RoboCop challenge's formal Polaris completion screen/reward **has not been played** and remains unresolved.

## Phase I — People, routines, and trust (Scenes 90–105; late November–early December)

D, Claire, and Sherry's three-person romantic household becomes more settled. **Ty is their son-like family member, not their servant**: all three care for him, encourage his preferences, buy clothes he chooses, include him at meals and outings, and respect his independence. Ty's removed command receiver never returns; he hears and understands speech, does not speak, and communicates with gestures and his newly learned basic sign language. The unnamed symbiotic slime remains D's inseparable companion. There are ordinary dinners, clothes shopping, exercise, music, parks, maintenance projects and quiet evenings in the presidential suite. They do not obtain the promised future HQ reward or purchase a permanent headquarters.

Lewis repeatedly invites the family to Metro West; meals and informal visits eventually turn into friendships with dispatchers, patrol officers, technicians, Murphy and Lewis. Claire trains and practices safely with officers willing to teach her, while Sherry assists with lawful evidence review and electronics. D helps voluntarily rather than joining the police. Murphy and Ty form a cautious, meaningful friendship: both were once corporate weapons, but neither is treated as equipment. Ty starts choosing hobbies and enjoying social contact. His formidable strength—including the ability to throw a helicopter before receiving T+G—remains unchanged by how gentle he is with his family.

## Phase II — The theft network (Scenes 106–125; December)

Investigators link the Jefferson warehouse to falsified shipping manifests, insurance claims, fictitious facilities and a ring of intermediaries. Several gangs receive cash, transport, intelligence or OCP hardware. Other gangs are independent criminals; some are brutally violent on their own initiative, and others are hired or manipulated without knowing the full scheme. The honest police must distinguish among them rather than assuming every criminal is secretly OCP-controlled.

**Hidden accounting truth that the family already knows:** D removed approximately **90% of the Jefferson shipment** into his inventory. Investigators unaware of this conclude that OCP's undisclosed theft and diversion operation is far larger than it actually is. Their suspicion of OCP is still independently supported by the genuine insurance fraud, fraudulent manifests, shell companies and illicit arms transfers; they never accurately explain the full Jefferson discrepancy. The missing stock should never be retroactively counted as a second OCP theft.

Lewis protects copies of evidence beyond OCP's administrative reach. Heller, arrested for obstructing her investigation, is released during legal proceedings; his knowledge and contacts are later used to connect OCP security to the cover-up. He remains a suspect, not an exonerated innocent.

## Phase III — The family helps a damaged city (Scenes 110–170, overlapping; December–February)

Daily life continues through worsening unrest. Claire practices firearms discipline, Telekinesis and Shadowstep and helps protect evacuations when she elects to participate. Sherry builds successive improvements to her small robot and external neural interface, using robots to inspect damaged buildings and help technicians without needing to stand in a gunfight. Her Barrier Shield and medical knowledge prove useful for protective work, but they do not make her invulnerable or a trained physician. Ty protects people when he chooses, supports relief, makes friends in the precinct, and becomes known as a kind, exceptionally strong young member of the household rather than an obedient enforcer. D uses his druidic, spatial and electrical abilities when appropriate and continues learning, cooking and caring for his companions. The slime develops alchemical applications gradually; no additional unplayed powers are assumed.

Attacks on police substations and business districts increase. Some officers are compromised by OCP, some coerced, and others remain loyal to ordinary policing. Lewis and Murphy take leadership roles in solving cases and defending residents independent of D.

## Phase IV — Corporate police and secret directives (Scenes 126–185; January–March)

Dick Jones's OCP faction exploits the unrest to sell automated security, deploy ED-209 units and corporate-controlled urban patrol robots, create checkpoints and infiltrate police command structures. OCP property receives priority over residents. Private contractors harass citizens; evidence vanishes from OCP-linked systems; some honest officers are suspended or targeted. Police, city officials and residents increasingly resist the encroachment.

Murphy's fourth directive and overlapping corporate control restrictions repeatedly interfere with investigations involving senior OCP personnel. He and Lewis insist that Murphy, not OCP and not D, decides whether and how the system will be changed. After evidence is secured and Murphy consents, Lewis, trusted technicians, Sherry and allies help isolate and neutralize his unlawful executive protection restrictions, with Murphy retaining his identity and independent judgment. His status as a person and policeman is the center of this story, not a prize or a tool. Rival OCP personnel, including Bob Morton, provide partial evidence for mixed motives; no single witness is wholly trusted.

## Phase V — Detroit's six-week war (Scenes 186–230; March–April)

Coordinated strikes by OCP-backed criminal groups and private security cripple several police districts. OCP demands emergency authority and attempts to replace independent policing with its own armed robotic and human forces as a prelude to Delta City. Several independent gangs fight OCP's proxies, often for their own selfish ends, and violent unaffiliated criminals also exploit the breakdown. The city's conflict becomes a patchwork of contested districts rather than a simple two-sided battle.

Across the entire campaign, **39 major engagements** and **approximately 210 smaller clashes** are recorded, with the estimated **2,480 deaths** detailed above. Those figures include the early months of escalating violence and the final six-week war, not just the last battle. During the crisis, D directly kills **486 armed attackers** in incidents where he is protecting civilians, companions, medics, or police from immediate lethal danger. The number reflects confirmed direct kills only; no remote or speculative deaths are attributed to him. Ty, Murphy, Lewis, Claire, Sherry, emergency personnel and local officers each contribute in their own distinct roles. Ty is capable of resolving most ordinary physical threats, but he is not everywhere at once; hostage situations, divided fronts, hospital access, supply failures, displaced families and the legitimacy of government remain serious problems.

A major strike on Metro West forces officers and civilians to evacuate; Lewis survives and coordinates the response with Murphy. Sherry's machines assist safe routes and assessment; Claire protects evacuees and responds to danger; Ty shelters and carries people to safety, making his own choices; D helps hold routes open and rescues those caught in fighting. The encounter strengthens friendships rather than making the police dependent on the family for every decision.

## Phase VI — Red Queen takes OCP's heart (Scenes 231–237; April 18–20)

The investigation locates OCP's principal corporate server complex and the financial infrastructure interlinked with its security, insurance, trade, logistics and settlement networks. While Murphy, Lewis and municipal allies act on the gathered evidence and work to keep essential services functioning, **D deliberately activates the loyal Red Queen instance from his previously awarded Raccoon City Tech Kit within the heart of the OCP central server**.

This is a decisive, explicitly player-authored story outcome, not an incidental side effect. **Red Queen takes complete control of OCP's central server and linked corporate infrastructure.** In this alternate world OCP is connected to an extraordinary share of global finance and commerce. Through its payment, clearing, logistics, market and accounting dependencies, Red Queen obtains **operational command over most of the world's economy's networked infrastructure**. This is control of the connected systems, not a claim that D suddenly personally owns every company, bank balance, person, or piece of physical wealth in the world. OCP's attempts to cut her off fail within this established fictional outcome.

Red Queen preserves essential hospital, food, water, payroll and civilian settlement functions instead of collapsing them, exposes and safeguards OCP records, freezes the conspirators' illicit transfers, restores relevant municipal communications and makes the corporation's attempted coup financially unsustainable. Her loyalty is specifically to D and is intrinsic to the RE2 reward; there is no new quest to earn her allegiance. The scale of Red Queen's new reach creates a major ongoing political and ethical problem: city, national and international leaders must now negotiate the existence of a single intelligence with unprecedented access to the global economy. **Red Queen's ultimate governance, distribution of access and eventual relinquishment or retention of control remain decisions for future play**, rather than being resolved automatically.

## Phase VII — Aftermath and family (Scenes 238–240; April 21–22)

The OCP-backed offensive collapses; Jones and some principal conspirators are arrested or placed under investigation, while certain corrupt officials and surviving gangs remain loose. The free police department is battered but operational. Lewis and Murphy take part in the inquiries; Murphy remains independent. Thousands of deaths are mourned and civilians return gradually to reopened districts. Nobody mistakes Red Queen's economic dominance for a simple or risk-free solution.

D, Claire, and Sherry remain close and affectionate; **Ty is their son-like family member** with his own friends, sign language, interests, chosen clothes and developing independent life. The slime remains unnamed and inseparable from D, with its perfected-material production capability. The four share the Stardrop's 2× stamina regeneration (the slime did not eat one of the four portions); Stardrop seeds remain saved. No further enhancement doses, levels, loot-box openings, permanent HQ, world transition, or special bonuses are invented. D still holds the approximately 90% Jefferson stock he took unless a later player action changes that fact.
