# Gotham: The Nature of the City, Its Heroes and Its Villains

> **Story Note: Worthy of Death**
>
> - Looking at a person is enough to roughly tell if they are worthy of death.
> - The vast majority of grunts and villains are worthy of death.
> - When a grunt dies, there is no heartbreak.
> - This rule exists to justify any deaths that happen in the story.

## Why Gotham Never Stops Fighting

Gotham works less like a real city and more like a pressure cooker built to produce conflict.

- **The city itself is sick.** Corruption runs through the police, the courts, City Hall and the old-money families. In some versions it is supernatural: the Court of Owls has secretly ruled for centuries, Ra's al Ghul keeps trying to "cleanse" it and stories like *Gates of Gotham* tie the darkness to the architecture and bloodlines.
- **Batman treats symptoms, not causes.** Bruce fights crime on rooftops instead of fixing the systems that breed it. Out of universe, a solved Gotham ends the story. In universe, Batman is a trauma response to one night in Crime Alley, relived forever.
- **Escalation.** Once a man in a costume appears, criminals start wearing costumes too. Many rogues are dark mirrors of Batman's own obsession.
- **The revolving door.** The no-kill rule plus a broken Arkham Asylum and Blackgate Penitentiary means every villain eventually returns.
- **Myth over realism.** Batman is closer to Sisyphus than a cop. Gotham is never saved. It is only defended, one night at a time.

## The Heroes: Batman and the Bat Family

### Batman (Bruce Wayne)
Orphaned at eight when Joe Chill shot Thomas and Martha Wayne in Crime Alley. Spent years training worldwide in martial arts, detective work, science and escapology. No superpowers, just intellect, discipline, wealth and preparation. His two unbreakable rules are no killing and no guns. He is called the World's Greatest Detective and is famous for having a contingency plan for everyone, including his own allies. Bruce Wayne the playboy is the mask. Batman is the real person.

### Alfred Pennyworth
The Wayne family butler and Bruce's surrogate father. Usually a former British soldier or intelligence operative, which makes him a field medic, tactician and mechanic. He is the moral anchor of the family and the one person who can tell Bruce he is wrong. Several stories treat him as the true heart of the Batcave.

### Commissioner James Gordon
One of the few honest cops in Gotham. Rose from lieutenant to commissioner largely alongside Batman's career. Operates the Bat-Signal and walks the line between legal authority and a vigilante partnership. Father of Barbara Gordon (and in some continuities, the psychopath James Gordon Jr.).

### Dick Grayson (Robin I / Nightwing)
Circus acrobat whose parents, the Flying Graysons, were murdered by a mob boss's sabotage. Bruce took him in as the first Robin. Grew up, clashed with Bruce over control and became Nightwing, protecting the nearby city of Blüdhaven. Widely seen as the most well-adjusted member of the family and the one everyone else connects through. Leader of the Teen Titans in many eras and has filled in as Batman more than once.

### Jason Todd (Robin II / Red Hood)
Street kid caught stealing the Batmobile's tires. Angrier and more violent than Dick. Murdered by the Joker in *A Death in the Family* (readers voted by phone for his death). Resurrected through the Lazarus Pit and returned as the Red Hood, a gun-carrying vigilante who kills. He is the living argument against Batman's no-kill rule and the family's most painful wound.

### Tim Drake (Robin III / Red Robin)
Deduced Batman's and Nightwing's identities on his own as a kid. Became Robin because he believed Batman needed one to stay balanced after Jason's death. The detective of the younger generation and often considered the closest to Bruce intellectually. Later became Red Robin.

### Damian Wayne (Robin V)
Bruce's biological son with Talia al Ghul, raised by the League of Assassins. Arrogant, lethal and highly trained from birth. His arc is learning compassion and the no-kill code. Killed and later resurrected. Often paired with Dick Grayson's Batman during Dick's time in the cowl.

### Stephanie Brown (Spoiler / Robin IV / Batgirl)
Daughter of the minor villain Cluemaster. Became Spoiler to sabotage her father's crimes. Briefly the fourth Robin, later Batgirl. Known for optimism and humor in a grim family.

### Barbara Gordon (Batgirl / Oracle)
Commissioner Gordon's daughter. Became Batgirl on her own initiative. Shot and paralyzed by the Joker in *The Killing Joke*, she reinvented herself as Oracle, the information broker and hacker who coordinated heroes across the DC universe and led the Birds of Prey. Later continuities restored her mobility and returned her to Batgirl.

### Cassandra Cain (Batgirl / Orphan)
Daughter of assassins David Cain and Lady Shiva. Raised without spoken language so she could read body language perfectly, making her arguably the best hand-to-hand fighter in the family. Killed a man as a child and swore never to kill again. Later operated as Orphan.

### Kate Kane (Batwoman)
Bruce's cousin on his mother's side. Former military cadet discharged under "Don't Ask, Don't Tell." Operates with her father's military support and a more soldier-like approach than Bruce.

### Duke Thomas (Signal)
Gotham teen with light-based metahuman powers. Works the daytime shift, protecting Gotham while the rest of the family sleeps.

### Selina Kyle (Catwoman)
Both ally and adversary. A cat burglar with her own moral code who has been Bruce's lover, fiancée and rival. Protects the East End and its outcasts. Sits on the line between hero and villain more than anyone else in Gotham.

## The Villains: The Rogues Gallery

### The Joker
Batman's archenemy and the most famous villain in comics. No confirmed origin, by design. *The Killing Joke* offers a failed comedian who fell into chemicals but the Joker himself says he prefers his past "multiple choice." He embodies chaos against Batman's order. Responsible for Jason Todd's death, Barbara Gordon's paralysis and countless massacres. His obsession is not winning but proving that anyone can break with "one bad day." He sees Batman as his perfect counterpart and would not want to live in a world without him.

### Harley Quinn (Dr. Harleen Quinzel)
Arkham psychiatrist who fell for the Joker while treating him. Became his partner and victim in an abusive relationship. Later broke free and became an antihero, often allied with Poison Ivy. One of DC's most popular characters across comics, games and film.

### Two-Face (Harvey Dent)
Gotham's crusading district attorney and once Bruce's friend and ally. Half his face was scarred by acid thrown by a mobster. His existing split personality took over. Makes every major decision by flipping a scarred coin. He represents the fall of legitimate justice and Batman's greatest failure to save a friend.

### The Penguin (Oswald Cobblepot)
Gotham's most successful crime boss. Runs the Iceberg Lounge as a front. Mocked as a child for his appearance, he craves respect and high society acceptance. Uses trick umbrellas. More businessman than madman, which makes him one of the few villains Batman sometimes tolerates as a source of information.

### The Riddler (Edward Nygma)
Compulsively leaves riddles and clues at his crimes because he needs to prove he is the smartest person in the room. His compulsion is both his strength and his weakness. Has occasionally reformed as a private detective. *Hush* and *Zero Year* feature him as a major threat.

### Scarecrow (Dr. Jonathan Crane)
Former psychology professor obsessed with fear. Uses fear toxin to make victims hallucinate their worst terrors. A direct mirror of Batman, who also uses fear as a weapon against criminals.

### Poison Ivy (Dr. Pamela Isley)
Botanist transformed into a plant-human hybrid. Controls plants and produces toxins and pheromones. An eco-terrorist who values plant life above human life. Often cast as an antihero and Harley Quinn's partner.

### Mr. Freeze (Dr. Victor Fries)
Cryogenics scientist who must wear a refrigerated suit after a lab accident. Commits crimes to fund a cure for his terminally ill wife Nora, kept in cryogenic stasis. Reinvented by *Batman: The Animated Series* episode "Heart of Ice" into one of the most tragic villains in Gotham.

### Bane
Raised in the brutal prison Peña Duro. A genius strategist and fighter enhanced by the drug Venom. In *Knightfall* he released Arkham's inmates, wore Batman down and broke his back. One of the few villains to defeat Batman physically and mentally.

### Ra's al Ghul
Centuries-old leader of the League of Assassins who stays alive through the Lazarus Pits. An eco-extremist who wants to save the planet by culling humanity. Respects Batman deeply and calls him "Detective." Wants Bruce as his heir and husband to his daughter Talia.

### Talia al Ghul
Ra's daughter and mother of Damian Wayne. Torn between her love for Bruce and loyalty to her father. Later led Leviathan, her own global criminal organization.

### Killer Croc (Waylon Jones)
Born with a condition that gives him reptilian skin, which worsened into a monstrous form. Super strength and durability. Lives in Gotham's sewers. Often a brute but sometimes portrayed sympathetically as an outcast.

### Clayface (Basil Karlo and others)
Shapeshifting mud-like villain. Basil Karlo, a washed-up actor, is the most famous version. Can mimic anyone. Has also been portrayed as a tragic figure who briefly joined the Bat Family in *Detective Comics*.

### Mad Hatter (Jervis Tetch)
Obsessed with *Alice in Wonderland*. Uses mind-control technology hidden in hats. One of the more disturbing rogues in modern stories.

### Hush (Tommy Elliot)
Bruce's childhood friend who tried to kill his own parents for their money. Resents Bruce's life and orchestrated an elaborate revenge plot in *Hush*. Bandaged face, surgeon skills and knowledge of Bruce's identity make him especially dangerous.

### The Court of Owls
Secret society of wealthy Gotham families who have controlled the city since its founding. Use undead assassins called Talons. Introduced by Scott Snyder, they proved Gotham's corruption ran deeper than Batman ever realized.

### Black Mask (Roman Sionis)
Sadistic crime lord who wears a skull mask carved from his father's coffin. Known for extreme torture. Major gang boss in *War Games* and the *Arkham* games.

### Deathstroke, Deadshot and Other Visitors
Gotham also attracts mercenaries and assassins from outside. Deadshot never misses, Deathstroke is a super soldier tactician and both regularly clash with the Bat Family.

### The Mob Families
Carmine Falcone and Sal Maroni ran Gotham's organized crime before the costumed villains arrived. *The Long Halloween* shows the shift from mobsters to "freaks," a turning point in Gotham's history.

## The Big Picture

Batman's rogues gallery is often called the best in comics because almost every villain reflects part of Batman himself:

| Villain | What They Reflect |
|---|---|
| Joker | Chaos versus Batman's order |
| Two-Face | Justice corrupted by trauma |
| Riddler | Intellect turned into obsession |
| Scarecrow | Fear used as a weapon |
| Bane | Body and mind pushed to the limit |
| Ra's al Ghul | A mission without mercy |
| Hush | The Bruce Wayne who chose hate |
| Court of Owls | The old Gotham elite Bruce was born into |

The heroes reflect Bruce too. Each Robin and Batgirl came from loss and was shaped into a soldier. The Bat Family exists because one man could never hold Gotham alone, and the villains exist because Gotham never stops producing them.

## The Arkham Games: Gotham's Villains in the Rocksteady/WB Montreal Series

The Arkham series (Origins 2013, Asylum 2009, City 2011, Knight 2015 in story order) tells one continuous war that shows the "why always fighting" theme better than almost any other version. Each game ends with Gotham worse off than before, and each victory sets up the next escalation.

### How the Comic Villains Play Out in Arkham

| Villain | Arkham Role | Fate in the Series |
|---|---|---|
| Joker | Main villain of Origins, Asylum and City. Haunts Knight as a hallucination in Bruce's mind | Dies of Titan poisoning in City. His blood infects Bruce and others, so he lives on as a hallucination in Knight |
| Harley Quinn | Joker's lieutenant in Asylum and City. Leads her own gang and attacks the GCPD in Knight | Survives. Grieves the Joker and runs her own crew |
| Two-Face | Gang boss in City. Bank robber in Knight | Wins the Arkham City gang war by default once Joker dies and Penguin falls. Jailed in Knight |
| Penguin | Arms dealer in Origins. Gang boss in City. Gunrunner in Knight | Locked in his own museum at the end of City. Jailed again in Knight |
| Riddler | Leaves trophies and puzzles in every game. Builds robots and race tracks in Knight | Captured every game and never stops |
| Scarecrow | Fear-gas boss in Asylum. Main villain of Knight | Apparently eaten by Croc in Asylum. Returns to evacuate Gotham with fear toxin in Knight and is defeated |
| Bane | Assassin and mercenary leader in Origins. Titan-pumped boss in Asylum | Defeated in both games. Absent from Knight's main story |
| Ra's al Ghul | Hidden mastermind behind Hugo Strange in City | Dies in City. Returns through the Lazarus Pit in Knight's Season of Infamy and dies for good |
| Talia al Ghul | Joker's hostage and Bruce's love in City | Shot and killed by the Joker in City |
| Hugo Strange | Warden of Arkham City and Protocol 10's architect | Killed by Ra's after failing |
| Mr. Freeze | Holds Nora's cure hostage in City. Side story in Knight | Gets a final side story in Knight's Season of Infamy |
| Killer Croc | Sewer boss in Asylum. Assassin in Origins | Escapes and later taken in by Amanda Waller in Knight's Season of Infamy |
| Poison Ivy | Titan-powered boss in Asylum. Ally against Scarecrow in Knight | Sacrifices herself to save Gotham from the fear toxin in Knight |
| Hush (Tommy Elliot) | Side villain in City. Impersonates Bruce Wayne in Knight | Captured in Knight |
| Black Mask | Gotham's crime boss in Origins (the Joker steals his identity). Drug lord in City | Survives and keeps running drugs |
| Arkham Knight (Jason Todd) | Main villain of Knight. Commands a private army | Turns back to help Bruce and becomes the Red Hood |
| Deathstroke | Lead assassin in Origins. Militia commander in Knight | Defeated and captured |
| Professor Pyg, Firefly, Man-Bat, Azrael, Deacon Blackfire and others | Side villains of Knight | All captured or resolved in Most Wanted missions |

The Court of Owls is the one major Gotham faction missing from the Arkham series. It appears in *Gotham Knights* (2022), which runs in its own separate continuity.

They are built from how many enemies appear across the map, the story missions and side activities, and the setting itself (Arkham City holds thousands of inmates). Rocksteady said Arkham Knight runs three to four times as many active thugs as Arkham City at any moment, with riots of up to 50 characters at once. Knight's militia also mans 21 watchtowers in Occupy Gotham alone, on top of checkpoints, tanks and drones.

### How the Arkham Series Fits the Gotham Pattern

The Arkham games prove the "always fighting" rule in a straight line. Batman stops the Joker in Origins and Asylum, so the city builds Arkham City. Arkham City creates a gang war and Protocol 10. The Joker's death opens a power vacuum. Every surviving villain then pools money behind Scarecrow and the Arkham Knight, and that ends with Gotham evacuated, Batman's identity exposed and the Knightfall Protocol. Every win clears the board for a bigger threat, which is exactly how Gotham works in the comics.
