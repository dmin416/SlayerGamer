## Origin and appearance

- Ty is the separately summoned T-103 / Mr. X companion, not the original Tyrant from Raccoon City, who was destroyed.
- His initial form recalls Mr. X's large, imposing frame and coat. Later perfected-virus changes give him healthy light-olive skin. He has preferences about clothing, appearance and presentation, and can choose what he wants to wear.
- He is extremely formidable even before later biological enhancements. Do not write him as weak or dependent upon later upgrades to be capable.

## Power baseline

**Sources (combined, not one game alone):** *Resident Evil 2 Remake* (T-00 / Mr. X stalker and Super Tyrant), *Resident Evil: Damnation* (T-103 family in open combat), Resident Evil wiki / franchise Tyrant material, and **whatever this story establishes on the page**. When those conflict, story decisions win.

**Floor, not a soft “weak until upgraded” reading:**

- **Massive strength** — far past human; throws, punches through masonry, overpowers groups; helicopter-scale feats are in the later continuity notes as pre-virus T-103 capability.
- **Massive durability** — small arms stagger or delay; they do not casually kill the coated or open-fight forms. Keep fighting after hits that drop ordinary people.
- **Recovery / tenacity** — stands back up; keeps going after punishment that would end a human fight.
- **Armor / limiter gear** — coat, hat, gloves as T-103 presentation and power limiting when intact; body and hide remain extreme with or without the coat once the story has him that far.
- **Electricity / bioelectricity** — later continuity adds bioelectricity and learned Chain Lightning; treat electrical capability as established once those beats are on the page, not as a reason he was frail before.
- **Open-ground combat** — Damnation-style: streets, halls, multiple threats; not limited to RPD corridors.
- **Adaptation** — later perfected T+G notes add open-ended biological adaptation; do not invent unlimited new powers without a scene.

Do not nerf him into a dependent bodyguard who needs constant upgrades to matter. Upgrades change presentation and open new tricks; they are not the start of his threat level.

Detail reference for remake / Damnation body stages: `Notes\References\Tyrant Capabilities.md`.

## Personality, expression and treatment

- Understands speech but does not speak. He communicates through posture, gestures, expressions, and later basic sign language.
- Do not give him voiced dialogue, inner monologues, or fluent sign-language conversation beyond what the story establishes.
- His desires and choices matter. He can be curious, tentative, protective, sociable and interested in ordinary experiences without becoming a robotic yes-man.
- In later Detroit continuity, D, Claire and Sherry treat Ty as their son-like family member. He is not a servant, slave, owned weapon or permanently assigned bodyguard.
- Everyday life matters: clothing, meals, recreation, hobbies, friendships and growing independence are as valid for him as action scenes.
- Murphy and Ty develop their own cautious friendship; each retains personal agency.
- Continuity correction: D physically removed Ty's implanted command receiver during his first transformation. It is absent thereafter; do not revert to an inactive receiver still in his body.
- Nonverbal communication can carry whole scenes without the narrator translating every gesture into a long explanation.
- Ty has an independent life and makes choices.
